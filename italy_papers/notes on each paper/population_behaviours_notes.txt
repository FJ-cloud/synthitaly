# 0. What this paper can and cannot define

The paper is a **payment-behaviour survey**, not a full income/wealth survey. It can define:

| Can define from paper                                          | Cannot define from paper                  |
| -------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Payment method probabilities                                   | Full income distribution of Italy         |
| Payment method by amount                                       | Starting bank balances                    |
| Payment method by age, gender, education, income group, region | Starting wallet cash distribution         |
| POS / online / P2P / recurring payment composition             | Total wealth or savings                   |
| Merchant/payment acceptance effects                            | Exact daily purchase frequency per person |
| Access to cards, accounts, instant payments, cash, crypto      | Household composition                     |

The survey was conducted between September 2023 and June 2024. For Italy it contains **4,088 respondents**, **8,050 POS transactions**, **397 P2P transactions**, **2,837 online transactions**, and **11,825 recurring payments**. POS, P2P, and online payments were collected in a one-day diary; recurring payments refer to the previous 30 days.

# 5. POS purchase purpose and merchant acceptance

Payment behaviour differs by location and purpose.

| POS purpose/location       | Payment behaviour                        |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Home services              | Cash-heavy: 69% of value                 |
| Other services             | Cash-heavy: 67% of value                 |
| Pick-up station            | Cash-heavy: 65% of value                 |
| Travel                     | Cards dominate: 65% of value             |
| Taxes/public charges       | Cards dominate: 64% of value             |
| Other purchases            | Cards dominate: 54% of value             |
| Durable goods              | Cards dominate: 51% of value             |
| Street purchases           | Mobile relatively relevant: 15% of value |
| Vending/ticketing machines | Mobile relatively relevant: 8% of value  |

Merchant acceptance is a behavioural constraint. Cashless acceptance at POS is **87%**, up from **80%** in 2022. Travel and culture/entertainment have the highest cashless acceptance, around **96%**. Pick-up stations and home services have the lowest cashless acceptance, around **76–77%**. The paper also reports that **12%** of respondents faced at least one card/mobile refusal, and **6%** reported that cashless instruments were not accepted below a certain transaction value. 

This means actual payment behaviour is conditional on:

```python
P(payment_method | amount, agent_attributes, purchase_type, merchant_acceptance)
```

not only on consumer preference.

# 12. Correlations / associations described in the paper

The paper mostly reports **descriptive associations**, not regression coefficients. The main behavioural relationships are:

| Relationship                               | Direction                                                          |
| ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Transaction amount → payment method        | Higher amount reduces cash use and increases card/mobile/other use |
| Low-value POS purchases → cash             | Cash dominates especially below €50                                |
| Older age → cash                           | 65+ has highest POS cash use                                       |
| Younger age → mobile/digital               | 18–39 more likely to use mobile payments                           |
| Lower education → cash                     | Primary/lower-secondary group is more cash-heavy                   |
| Lower income → cash preference/use         | Up to €1,000 group has highest cash preference and high cash use   |
| Higher income → cashless preference        | More than €4,000 group has 75.2% cashless preference               |
| Merchant acceptance → actual method        | Cashless preference does not always translate into cashless use    |
| Online context → card/e-payment            | Online payments are mostly cards, then e-payment solutions         |
| P2P context → cash or card/mobile          | Cash leads by number, cards/mobile lead by value                   |
| Credit transfer → high-value P2P/online    | Credit transfers have much higher average transaction values       |
| Recurring payments → direct debit          | Direct debit dominates most recurring bills                        |
| Privacy concern → digital-payment friction | 58% report privacy concerns around digital payments                |
| Cash access → cash availability            | ATM access remains the central cash-supply mechanism               |

The strongest modelling relationship in the whole paper is:

```text
payment_method = f(transaction_context, transaction_amount, merchant_acceptance,
                   age, income_group, education, occupation, instrument_access)
```

The paper does not provide enough evidence to define:

```text
starting_balance = f(age, income, education, region)
```

or

```text
monthly_income = f(agent_type)
```

It only gives **income brackets used for behavioural conditioning**, not a full distribution of income or balances.
