§1.1

Purpose

To generate a synthetic, account-level consumer-transaction stream for Italy — salaries, bills, purchases and bank fees — calibrated to public Italian statistics where possible and explicitly flagged where not, for use in studying consumer financial behaviour and as a transparent teaching and prototyping dataset.

The model is framed as what a bank sees on its retail customers’ accounts.

Standard reference: Grimm et al. (2010), A standard protocol for describing individual-based and agent-based models, Ecological Modelling 221(23). The full protocol is docs/ODD.md; the why is in EXPLANATION.md and the value tables are in MODEL_REFERENCE.md.

§1.2

Four kinds of entity, of which exactly one decides anything.

Merchant

§1.3

Process overview and scheduling

Each day (ItalyModel.step), in this order:

  1. IncomeSource agents act. On payday — the 27th — each credits every consumer it serves, with the December 13th-month bonus (tredicesima) for payroll and pension.
  2. Consumer agents act in random order (shuffle_do). Each consumer, per day: pays any bills due, deferring unaffordable ones to the overdue queue; settles overdue bills when cash allows, with a late-payment penalty; makes discretionary purchases with a probability scaled by the day’s intensity (weekday / season / holiday × the payday spike); on the debt-service day — the 25th — services its debt by archetype; and at month-end sweeps any positive residual to savings or pension.
  3. The model records the day’s KPIs and the grouped balance snapshot, collects the DataCollector, and advances the clock by one day.

Scheduling is synchronous across days, randomised within a day. Income is paid before consumers act, so a consumer paid today can spend today.

§2

Design concepts

ConceptHow it applies here
Basic principlesDouble-entry, conserved accounting — every flow is a paired debit and credit. SHIW / Bank-of-Italy calibration of structure, with a behavioural-finance overlay (Olafsson & Pagel; Stango & Zinman; Dahan & Nisan) for within-cycle timing and fees.
EmergenceSavings are the month-end residual, not a rate. Fee incidence, the per-group balance trajectories, and the divergence of debtor outcomes — climbers clear, chronic stay stuck, subsisters drift up — all emerge from per-agent rules. None is imposed.
AdaptationMinimal and reactive. A consumer that cannot afford a bill defers it and pays it late with a penalty; a subsister borrows on a credit line to cover a shortfall. There is no optimisation and no foresight.
Objectives / learning / predictionNone. Agents follow fixed rules; they do not maximise a utility, learn, or predict.
SensingA consumer senses only its own balance and overdraft floor, to decide affordability, and the calendar — payday, bill days, holidays.
InteractionMediated entirely by payments: IncomeSource→Consumer (salary), Consumer→Merchant (purchases, bills), Consumer→bank stand-ins (fees, debt service), credit_line→Consumer (loans), and the internal current→savings/pension sweep.
StochasticityA single seeded NumPy Generator drives every draw: macro-area and income source, the income lognormal (located by both the source and the macro-area multiplier), the debt-participation and saver Bernoulli rolls, the vulnerability-tilted subtype draw, and the daily category / ticket / purchase draws. A fixed seed reproduces a run byte-for-byte (tests/test_schema.py).
CollectivesMacro-areas, income quartiles and quintiles, income levels, debtor subtypes, and the area × band × financial-status clusters used for grouping. Macro-area is now a causal collective rather than a reporting one: it sets the income level, and the income bands then drive debt, saving and vulnerability. Area differences in those outcomes are emergent — none is calibrated by area directly.
ObservationThe flat transactions ledger, the export_accounts() snapshot, the per-day DataCollector series, and eleven live Solara panels.
§3.1–3.2

Initialisation and input data

Initialisation — deterministic given the seed

ItalyModel.__init__ builds, in order:

  1. The merchant pools and the bill / fee / credit-line stand-ins.
  2. One IncomeSource per area.
  3. N consumers — each assigned a macro-area by population weight and a primary income source, then an income drawn from that source’s own lognormal, scaled by both the source and the macro-area multiplier (each mean-preserving, so the population mean is undisturbed), plus a one-month starting buffer and a bill subscription set.
  4. Income bands — quartile and quintile from the realised percentiles; level from the absolute-euro PBS bands.
  5. Debt — a SHIW participation roll by quartile; debt-holders get a service amount, a vulnerability flag, a vulnerability-tilted subtype, an opening principal, and for chronic a standing overdraft.
  6. Savings flags are rolled.
  7. The visual graph and the DataCollector.

Savings, fees and debt trajectories are not initialised. They emerge.

Input data

No external inputs

The model reads no external input files or time series at run time. Every parameter is a constant in numbers.py, each traceable to a paper or flagged as a modelling choice. The only “input” is the calendar, computed from the start date.

§3.3

Submodels, each with its source

Every submodel names its key parameter and where that parameter comes from. ⚠ marks a value the protocol declares to be a modelling choice rather than a calibrated one — the honest half of the exercise.

Full value tables are in docs/MODEL_REFERENCE.md.
SubmodelRuleSource
Income payment
IncomeSource.step		 On the 27th, credit each served consumer their monthly_income; ×2 in December for payroll and pension. Each consumer has exactly one income stream. SHIW 2022; Semeraro et al. (2020) for the calendar
Per-source, per-area income draw
sample_income_for_source		 Lognormal with the source’s own dispersion INCOME_SOURCE_SIGMA, centred so its mean equals base_mean × income_source_multiplier(source) × macro_area_income_multiplier(area). Both multipliers are mean-preserving over their own dimension, so the population mean — and the SHIW quartile calibration — is undisturbed; what moves is the dispersion. SHIW 2022 §2B (source levels); Semeraro et al. (2020) p. 5 — ISTAT 2017 regional accounts, South GDP per capita 45% below Centre-North, corroborated p. 27 (wire transfers −44.6%) — for the area levels. σ = ⚠ proxy. The paper treats Centre-North as one bloc, so NORTH and CENTRE carry the same level.
Discretionary purchase
_maybe_buy_from_merchant		 With probability scaled by daily_intensity (weekday × month × holiday × Christmas × the mean-neutral payday-cycle multiplier), pick a category and a lognormal ticket and pay a random merchant in-area. Emiliozzi et al. (2023); payday spike = Olafsson & Pagel (2018), ⚠ magnitude
Bill payment & late fees
_pay_due_bills, _settle_overdue_bills		 Pay each subscribed bill on its due day if affordable; otherwise queue it and later settle with an 11% penalty; write off after 90 days. Payment Behaviour Survey §4 “Recurring payments”, print p. 16 / fig. A4.1; Dahan & Nisan (2020), ⚠
Overdraft fee
_charge_overdraft_fee		 A flat €30 the moment a payment takes the current account from ≥0 to <0. Stango & Zinman (2014), ⚠ magnitude
Debt service
_service_debt, day 25		 Accrue monthly interest on the principal, then repay by archetype — climber the full SHIW service (principal falls, then digs out and leaves debt); chronic interest-only (principal non-decreasing, never clears); subsister a token amount, and borrows elsewhere. SHIW 2022 §3 (service flow); stock, rate and archetypes = ⚠ choices; the chronic tilt is grounded in SHIW financial vulnerability
Borrowing
_borrow		 A subsister draws from the per-area credit line — a paired credit and debit, so money is conserved — bounded by a ceiling on the principal. ⚠ flagged
Month-close sweep
_month_close		 At month-end a saver moves its positive residual (income − bills − debt − discretionary, capped at the current balance) to savings, or to pension for a pension-saver. SHIW 2022 §2F (saver probability); the sweep itself is emergent — there is no savings-rate parameter
⚠ The sweep is also the leak

The month-close sweep is a debit on the current account, which is why cur_total_out and cur_balance silently encode is_saver and had to be quarantined. See Study D on the validation page — this row of the protocol and that result are the same fact seen from two directions.

One attribution to keep straight

⚠ Emiliozzi, not “Carlsen-Riccaboni”

The Italian credit-card paper was cited ≈47 times across this repository under a name that appears nowhere in the PDF. The correct citation is:

Emiliozzi, S., Rondinelli, C. & Villa, S. (2023). Consumption during the Covid-19 pandemic: evidence from Italian credit cards. Banca d’Italia, Questioni di Economia e Finanza (Occasional Papers) No. 769, May 2023.

Corrected across the active tree. This paper is the source for all ten category shares, ticket sizes and the whole seasonality calendar. Still open: the §3 / §5 / §6 / §9 / §11 section anchors attached to it throughout numbers.py were inherited from the bad draft and have never been verified against the real document — they point at working notes, not checked sections. Verify by hand before the thesis quotes a section number. The macro-area weight citation to its “§6” was one of these, and it was wrong; the weights are now ISTAT’s.
