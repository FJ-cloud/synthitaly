## 1. Overview

### 1.1 Purpose

To generate a **synthetic, account-level consumer-transaction stream for Italy** — salaries,
bills, purchases, and bank fees — that is calibrated to public Italian statistics where
possible and explicitly flagged where not, for use in studying consumer financial behaviour
and as a transparent teaching/prototyping dataset. The model is framed as *what a bank sees on
its retail customers' accounts*.

### 1.2 Entities, state variables, and scales

**Agents.**

- **Consumer** — a household; the only active decision-maker. State variables: `macro_area`;
  `monthly_income`; `income_source` ∈ {payroll, self_employed, pension, transfers, unemployed};
  `income_level` ∈ {low, middle, high}; `income_quartile` (1–4), `income_quintile` (1–5);
  debt state (`has_debt`, `debtor_subtype` ∈ {climber, chronic,
  subsister} or None, `debt_balance`, `_monthly_debt_service`, `debt_service_ratio`,
  `is_financially_vulnerable`, `overdraft_allowed`, `_overdraft_floor`); saving state
  (`is_saver`, `is_pension_saver`); three `BankAccount`s (`current`, `savings`, `pension`);
  `bills_subscribed`; monthly cash-flow accumulators; an `_overdue_bills` queue.
- **Merchant** — a passive payee in one (category, area); one `BankAccount` that only receives.
- **IncomeSource** — one per macro-area, the "employer"; pays its attached consumers on payday;
  its account runs negative by the total paid out.
- **Stand-in payees** — per-area `BankAccount`s for bills, `debt_service`, `overdraft_fee`, and
  the `credit_line` lender, so every consumer outflow/inflow has a counterparty.

**Collective / environment.** `ItalyModel` holds the daily clock (`today`), the population, a
`networkx` graph for the network panel, and a Mesa `DataCollector`. All empirical constants
live in `numbers.py`.

**Scales.** Time step = **one day**; default horizon 30 days (configurable; the model is run
up to ~2 years for debt dynamics). "Space" is non-geographic but not inert: **three
macro-areas** (NORTH / CENTRE / SOUTH) carry ISTAT population weights (0.46 / 0.20 / 0.34) and
an income level — Southern households are drawn at 0.554× the Centre-North level. There is no
distance, no coordinates and no within-area structure. Default population 200 consumers
(`model.py:731`); the thesis reference configuration uses 800. 10
spending categories × 3 areas × *k* merchants. Money is in euros.

### 1.3 Process overview and scheduling

Each day (`ItalyModel.step`), in order:

1. **IncomeSource agents act** — on payday (the 27th) each credits every consumer it serves
   (with the December 13th-month bonus for payroll/pension).
2. **Consumer agents act in random order** (`shuffle_do`). Each consumer, per day:
   pays any **bills** due (deferring unaffordable ones to the overdue queue); **settles overdue
   bills** when cash allows (with a late-payment penalty); makes **discretionary purchases**
   with a probability scaled by the day's intensity (weekday/season/holiday × the payday spike);
   on the debt-service day (the 25th) **services its debt** by archetype; and at month-end
   **sweeps** any positive residual to savings/pension.
3. The model records the day's KPIs and the grouped balance snapshot, **collects** the
   `DataCollector`, and advances `today` by one day.

Scheduling is **synchronous across days, randomised within a day**; income is paid before
consumers act so a consumer paid today can spend today.

## 2. Design concepts

- **Basic principles.** Double-entry, conserved accounting (every flow is a paired
  debit/credit); SHIW/Bank-of-Italy calibration of structure; a behavioural-finance overlay
  (Olafsson & Pagel; Stango & Zinman; Dahan & Nisan) for within-cycle timing and fees.
- **Emergence.** Savings (the month-end residual, **not** a rate), fee incidence, the per-group
  balance trajectories, and the divergence of debtor outcomes (climbers clear, chronic stay
  stuck, subsisters drift up) all emerge from per-agent rules — they are not imposed.
- **Adaptation.** Minimal and reactive: a consumer that cannot afford a bill **defers** it
  (and pays it late with a penalty later), and a subsister **borrows** on a credit line to
  cover a shortfall. There is no optimisation or foresight.
- **Objectives / learning / prediction.** None. Agents follow fixed rules; they do not maximise
  a utility, learn, or predict.
- **Sensing.** A consumer senses only its own account balance and overdraft floor (to decide
  affordability) and the calendar (payday, bill days, holidays).
- **Interaction.** Mediated entirely by payments: IncomeSource→Consumer (salary),
  Consumer→Merchant (purchases, bills), Consumer→bank stand-ins (fees, debt service),
  credit_line→Consumer (loans), and internal Consumer current→savings/pension sweeps.
- **Stochasticity.** A single seeded NumPy `Generator` drives every draw: macro-area and income
  source, the income lognormal (located by both the source and the macro-area multiplier), the
  debt-participation and saver Bernoulli rolls, the
  debtor-subtype draw (tilted by vulnerability), and the daily category/ticket/purchase draws.
  A fixed seed reproduces a run byte-for-byte (`tests/test_schema.py`).
- **Collectives.** Macro-areas, income quartiles/quintiles, income levels, debtor subtypes, and
  the area × band × financial-status clusters used for grouping and observation. Macro-area is
  now a *causal* collective rather than a reporting one: it sets the income level, and the
  income bands then drive debt, saving and vulnerability. Area differences in those outcomes
  are emergent — none of them is calibrated by area directly.
- **Observation.** The flat `transactions` ledger, the `export_accounts()` snapshot, the
  per-day `DataCollector` series (volume, debt-by-subtype, mean balance by source/level/subtype),
  and the eleven live Solara panels.

## 3. Details

### 3.1 Initialization

Deterministic given `seed`. `ItalyModel.__init__` builds, in order: the merchant pools and the
bill/fee/credit-line stand-ins; one IncomeSource per area; **N consumers**, each assigned a
macro-area (by population weight) and a primary income source, then an income drawn from that
source's own lognormal, scaled by both the source and the macro-area multiplier (each
mean-preserving, so the population mean is undisturbed), a
one-month starting buffer, and a bill subscription set. Then **income bands** are assigned
(quartile/quintile from the realised percentiles; level from the absolute-euro PBS bands),
**debt** is assigned (a SHIW participation roll by quartile; debt-holders get a service amount,
a vulnerability flag, a vulnerability-tilted subtype, an opening principal, and — for chronic —
a standing overdraft), and **savings** flags are rolled. Finally the visual graph and the
`DataCollector` are built. Savings, fees, and debt trajectories are **not** initialised — they
emerge.

### 3.2 Input data

The model uses **no external input files or time series at run time**. Every parameter is a
constant in [`numbers.py`](../src/synthitaly/numbers.py), each traceable to a paper or flagged
as a modelling choice (see [`MODEL_REFERENCE.md`](MODEL_REFERENCE.md)). The only "input" is the
calendar, computed from the start date.

### 3.3 Submodels

Each submodel names its key parameter(s) and source; full values are in `MODEL_REFERENCE.md`.

- **Income payment** (`IncomeSource.step`). On the 27th, credit each served consumer their
  `monthly_income`; ×2 in December for payroll/pension (the *tredicesima*). Each consumer has
  exactly one income stream. *Source: SHIW 2022; Semeraro et al. (2020) for the calendar.*
- **Per-source, per-area income draw** (`sample_income_for_source`). Lognormal with the
  source's own dispersion `INCOME_SOURCE_SIGMA`, centred so its mean equals
  `base_mean × income_source_multiplier(source) × macro_area_income_multiplier(area)`. Both
  multipliers are mean-preserving over their own dimension, so the population mean — and the
  SHIW quartile/quintile calibration — is undisturbed; what moves is the dispersion.
  *Source: SHIW 2022 §2B (source levels); Semeraro et al. (2020) p. 5 — ISTAT 2017 regional
  accounts, South GDP per capita 45% below Centre-North, corroborated p. 27 (wire transfers
  received by natural persons −44.6%) — for the area levels; σ = ⚠ flagged proxy. The paper
  treats Centre-North as one bloc, so NORTH and CENTRE carry the same level.*
- **Discretionary purchase** (`_maybe_buy_from_merchant`). With probability scaled by
  `daily_intensity` (weekday × month × holiday × Christmas × the mean-neutral payday-cycle
  multiplier), pick a category and a lognormal ticket, and pay a random merchant in-area.
  *Source: Emiliozzi et al. (2023); payday spike = Olafsson & Pagel (2018), ⚠ magnitude.*
- **Bill payment & late fees** (`_pay_due_bills`, `_settle_overdue_bills`). Pay each subscribed
  bill on its due day if affordable; otherwise queue it and later settle with an 11% penalty;
  write off after 90 days. *Source: Payment Behaviour Survey §4 "Recurring payments", print
  p. 16 / fig. A4.1; Dahan & Nisan (2020), ⚠.*
- **Overdraft fee** (`_charge_overdraft_fee`). A flat €30 the moment a payment takes the current
  account from ≥0 to <0. *Source: Stango & Zinman (2014), ⚠ magnitude.*
- **Debt service** (`_service_debt`, day 25). Accrue monthly interest on the principal; then
  repay by archetype — **climber** the full SHIW service (principal falls, then digs out and
  leaves debt); **chronic** interest-only (principal non-decreasing, never clears); **subsister**
  a token amount (and borrows elsewhere). *Source: SHIW 2022 §3 (service flow); stock, rate,
  archetypes = ⚠ flagged choices; chronic tilt grounded in SHIW financial vulnerability.*
- **Borrowing** (`_borrow`). A subsister draws from the per-area `credit_line` (a paired
  credit/debit, so money is conserved), bounded by a ceiling on the principal. *⚠ flagged.*
- **Month-close sweep** (`_month_close`). At month-end a saver moves its positive residual
  (income − bills − debt − discretionary, capped at the current balance) to savings, or pension
  for a pension-saver. *Source: SHIW 2022 §2F (saver probability); the sweep itself is emergent,
  no savings-rate parameter.*
