# Figure captions

- **f01_txn_volume** — Daily transaction count (top) and total euro throughput (bottom) over a 120-day run — the raw output stream. The four spikes are the 27th of each month, when salaries, pensions and transfers are credited.
- **f02_spend_mix_vs_paper** — Simulated category shares against the Emiliozzi et al. (2023) card-data baseline the model is calibrated to. The match is directional, not exact — the largest gaps are Travel 8% against 9%; Food & groceries 21% against 20%.
- **f03_spend_by_area** — Cumulative card spend per macro-area: North €5,489,398, Centre €2,452,947, South €3,502,855. The South out-spends the Centre on population weight alone despite the lower per-household income.
- **f04_payday_spike** — Daily purchase euro with the payday rules marked. Spending bunches just after the 27th and decays through the month — a mean-neutral re-timing, so monthly totals are unaffected.
- **f05_behavioural_events** — Daily overdraft (Stango & Zinman) and late-payment (Dahan & Nisan) fee events. Late-payment charges spike on bill dates; overdraft charges form a thin continuous tail — the liquidity-constrained minority.
- **f06_income_composition** — Consumer headcount per income source, each bar split low / middle / high. Payroll dominates; the high band is confined to payroll and self-employment, and no transfers or unemployed household reaches it.
- **f07_balance_by_source** — Mean current-account balance per income source. The payday sawtooth and the persistent sub-zero floor for unemployed households emerge from the interaction of monthly credits with daily spending; neither is imposed.
- **f08_income_distribution** — Per-source monthly net income distributions. Self-employment is both the highest-paying and the widest-spread; pensions are the tightest; transfers and unemployment sit lowest with almost no dispersion.
- **f09_debt_stock_by_subtype** — Total outstanding debt principal per archetype over roughly two years. Climbers pay down steadily but flatten at €89,101 rather than reaching zero — the residual of the households that never clear (see f12). Chronic debt is near-flat at €277,144; subsister debt drifts up to €319,362.
- **f10_balance_by_subtype** — Mean current balance per archetype. Climbers rebuild a rising buffer once their principal is paid down; chronic households track them at a lower level; subsisters return to zero at the end of every month throughout.
- **f11_debtor_composition** — Debtors split into climber / chronic / subsister. The darker base of each bar is the SHIW financially-vulnerable cohort that the chronic tilt targets: 17 of 44 chronic debtors, against 9 of 37 subsisters and 7 of 86 climbers.
- **f12_still_in_debt** — Households still carrying debt principal, by archetype. 67 of the 86 climbers reach zero, all of them between day 390 and day 420; the remaining 19 do not. No chronic or subsister household clears at any point in the run.
- **f13_clustering_pca** — Debtors in the plane of their first two principal components. Unsupervised KMeans clusters (left) against the true archetypes (right). The subsister group separates cleanly; climber and chronic overlap heavily, which is what caps the recovery scores in f14.
- **f14_cluster_recovery** — Cluster-recovery scores and the archetype-by-cluster confusion matrix. Adding the debt-mechanic proxies lifts ARI from 0.21 to 0.36 and NMI from 0.19 to 0.38 — most of the apparent structure comes from those features, not from behaviour.
- **f15_prediction** — Debtor prediction: honest behavioural features reach AUC 0.674 (± 0.067 across folds); adding the debt-mechanic proxies leaks the label (1.000 ± 0.000). Five-fold stratified CV on the full 34-column honest set, with the five fold curves shown under their vertical average. The annotated AUC is the per-fold mean, the same estimator validation_report.md pins, so the two agree. The coefficient panel shows the 14 largest of 34 by absolute size.
- **f16_saver_rate_by_quintile** — Share of households that save, by income quintile: realised (bars) against the SHIW 2022 §2F probability they were drawn from (line). Q2–Q4 of the target are linearly interpolated — SHIW reports only Q1 and Q5. Realised rates track the target within sampling error at n≈120 per quintile; the small uniform lift comes from subsisters, whose saver flag is force-set (see f19) and who are spread roughly evenly across quintiles rather than concentrated in the low ones.
- **f17_saver_balances_by_quintile** — End-of-run savings (left) and pension (right) balances among households that hold any, by income quintile. The two pots are exclusive — a pension-saver's residual is swept to pension instead of savings, never to both.
- **f18_saver_prediction** — Saver prediction needs three curves, not two: the column set this repo calls “fair” still reaches AUC 0.992 on this label, because the month-close sweep writes it into cur_total_out and cur_balance. Quarantining those two gives the honest 0.747 ± 0.040. Measured at the pinned 800 x 720 configuration on the full 32-column saver-fair set, as the per-fold mean AUC over five stratified folds — the same configuration and estimator validation_report.md pins, so the two agree.
- **f19_saver_debtor_confound** — Saver rate by debtor archetype. Subsisters are 100% savers because _assign_savings force-sets the flag for them — a deliberate choice in the debt layer that entangles the two labels. Every other archetype sits within a few points of the population mean of 55%. Declared here rather than corrected.
