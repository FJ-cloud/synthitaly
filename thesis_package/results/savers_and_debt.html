Part 1 · Debt

How the three archetypes are built

Who holds debt is a SHIW roll on income quartile. What kind of debtor they are is not in any paper — it is a modelling choice, tilted by SHIW's own financially-vulnerable definition so that the chronic cohort lands on genuinely distressed households rather than comfortable high earners.

c.has_debt              = numbers.has_debt(rng, c.income_quartile)      # SHIW
c._monthly_debt_service = numbers.annual_debt_service(rng, q) / 12.0    # SHIW
c.debt_service_ratio    = c._monthly_debt_service / c.monthly_income
c.is_financially_vulnerable = (q <= 2 and c.debt_service_ratio > 0.30)  # SHIW
c.debtor_subtype        = numbers.sample_debtor_subtype(rng, vulnerable)
c.debt_balance          = monthly_service * 12   # opening principal

The tilt: a vulnerable debtor is drawn 60% chronic, a resilient one 60% climber (numbers.py:238-247). Interest accrues at 0.5%/month whether or not the payment is made, so a skipped month grows the debt — the realistic direction.

The whole debt-as-stock layer is a modelling choice, and the code says so. SHIW gives debt participation and an annual debt-service flow. It never gives a principal, an interest rate, or a repayment archetype. Only the direction of the vulnerability tilt is paper-grounded — every magnitude here is ours, and is swept.

The bound on the debtor task is structural, and the fair result clears it. is_debtor is drawn by numbers.has_debt(), a Bernoulli on the income-quartile gradient {1: 0.120, 2: 0.192, 3: 0.244, 4: 0.285}, so at assignment time the income quartile is the only signal in the label and it caps the Bayes-optimal AUC at 0.603. Part 2 · Savers

How saving happens

There is no savings-rate parameter anywhere in the model. Saving is the residual of everything else, computed once a month:

residual = self._m_income - self._m_bills - self._m_debt - self._m_disc
if self.is_saver and residual > 0:
    amount = min(residual, self.account.balance)
    target = pension if self.is_pension_saver else savings
    self.account.debit(...); target.credit(...)      # paired, money conserved

So all the heterogeneity in savings comes from income minus obligations minus discretionary spend. A richer household with the same bills simply leaves a bigger residual. Whether a household sweeps at all is the SHIW roll below; how much is emergent.

The pension split

A saver passes a second independent roll of the same quintile probability to become a pension-saver. Their residual then goes to the pension pot instead of savings — never both.

Reusing a saving-participation probability as a pension-participation probability is an assumption. The code is candid that no contribution rate was invented, but the reuse itself is unjustified.

The forced subsister

_assign_savings force-sets is_saver = True for every subsister, so their surplus leaves the current account and it hugs zero.

This overrides SHIW for a whole cohort and entangles the two labels — see the confound section below.

Three of the five probabilities above are a linear interpolation, not survey output — SHIW reports only Q1 (0.70) and Q5 (0.28). The code marks them; the tier tables in the docs present the whole vector as calibrated.

Both

Two leaks, and why they are different

Each half of this page carries a column that looked innocent and was not. They failed in different ways, which is the interesting part.

Leak 1 — the debtor side: cur_n_entries

A raw count of current-account entries. Fair-sounding, and it silently included the debt-service, credit-draw and overdraft lines — re-importing the very leakage the LEAK_ prefix exists to quarantine.

Regressed on the other fair activity counts (R² = 0.975), its residual correlates +0.46 with the debt-mechanic line count and predicts is_debtor alone at AUC 0.78. Left in the fair set it inflated the headline from 0.91 to what should have been 0.697.

Leak 2 — the saver side: LEAK_SAVER

Fairness is relative to the label. The LEAK_ prefix quarantines what encodes debtor status; nothing was quarantining what encodes saver status.

The month-close sweep is a debit on the current account, so cur_total_out and cur_balance carry the saver label directly — worth AUC 0.995, a bigger leak than the first one.

The generalisable lesson. A feature is not fair because its name is innocuous — only if the generating process cannot write the label into it. Both leaks passed a name-based review and failed a mechanism-based one.

Where the two labels touch

The saver and debtor labels are not independent, and the reason is a single line in the debt layer (model.py:1092-1096).

Subsisters are 100% savers by construction. The forcing exists so a hand-to-mouth household's surplus leaves the current account and it hugs zero — a deliberate choice in the debt layer. But it means any analysis of the saver label is partly reading the debtor layer. Declared rather than corrected: hiding a known effect on a second label would be worse than reporting it.

One more check worth stating: savings balances are exactly zero for 100% of non-savers, which is the invariant that makes the sweep mechanic verifiable at all.

Both

What the pair says that neither says alone

Debtor

Clustering: partly (ARI 0.20 fair). Prediction: weakly (AUC 0.70 fair).

The label is drawn from a hidden flag, so behaviour cannot carry it. The ceiling is a property of the generator.

Saver

Clustering: no (ARI 0.008). Prediction: yes (AUC 0.83 fair).

The label is drawn from income quintile, which the ledger does reveal through income credits. It has an observable cause.

Clustering recovers a label only when that label aligns with a dominant axis of variation. Prediction only needs the signal to be present at all.

That is a statement about method rather than about this model, and it is the more transferable of the two findings — it is what the saver study is for, given that on its own it is a null result.

It also explains why honest saver prediction (0.83) beats honest debtor prediction (0.70) even though the saver study finds nothing by clustering: the saver label has an observable cause and the debtor subtype does not.
